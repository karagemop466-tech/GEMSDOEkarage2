04 / SCIENTIFIC INTERPRETATION

What may explain the GEMSDOE32 result?

The pinned GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 rasters are identical inside the mirror footprint. The GEMSDOE32 positive mask is a subset of GEMSDOE31 with 2,545 dots removed. All removed dots lie more than 1 and at most 2 pixels from the mirrored known-fault mask; none of the GEMSDOE32 dots lies within 2 pixels of it. GEMSDOE36 contains the GEMSDOE32 mask plus 1,200 dots. These are reproduced geometry facts on an unauthenticated mirror, not evidence of where hidden faults occur.

In the organizer's distance-weighted Tversky metric, nearby predictions can earn partial credit and false-positive mass is penalized. Sparse placement and removal of low-yield near-catalogue predictions are plausible reasons for an improvement. But removing points can also lose true-positive credit, and known-fault pixels—not an entire neighborhood—are excluded according to DrivenData staff. Hidden truth and authenticated receipts are unavailable. The reported 0.2778 is not a proven causal result or an organizer-confirmed file score, and nothing here establishes that a new file will exceed it.

Is there a demonstrated improvement? The upstream H48 run card reports a proxy gain, not independently reproduced here: HOLDOUT-DTI, GEMSDOE32-HIDE-RECOVER-v3.0, 22,232 withheld positives, 0.115341 [95% CI 0.100168, 0.130524] versus H33 0.099202 [0.085951, 0.112668]; paired bootstrap mean +0.016217 [0.008599, 0.024287]. The H38 card reports a higher point estimate, HOLDOUT-DTI on the same evaluator and 22,232 positives: 0.116958 [0.100533, 0.132040]; no paired H48-vs-H38 interval is available. H48 also regressed on the secondary proxy: HOLDOUT-DTI, GEMSDOE32-SGMC-CAL-v3.0, 62,703 off-catalogue positives, 0.056798 [0.050345, 0.063015] versus H33 0.064121 [0.056730, 0.071649]. The H48 card blocks release on that regression and the literal lattice overlap. These are owner-reported holdouts, not organizer receipts or predictions of leaderboard performance.

05 / WHEN A FILE IS READY

How a future submission would work.

  1. Wait for the decision to change. One assigned lane must pass individual feature leakage canaries, buffered whole-segment hide-and-recover with fold-local catalogue features, and a matched current-best comparison with a paired 95% confidence interval. Surface and final dots must pass against the complete current registry.
  2. Validate the exact saved bytes automatically. Use the authenticated organizer example for CRS, shape, transform and footprint; check one float32 band, no NaN/Inf inside and all values in [0, 1]. The local strict validator also requires NaN outside the footprint. This conservative interpretation of the published null/NaN wording is not proof of portal acceptance.
  3. Run the separate selector. Only an eligible candidate and the currently available weekly allowance can authorize a slot. The rules PDF describes a limit of three feedback submissions per week; confirm the current allowance in the account before submitting. No manual image inspector is used by these gates.
  4. Download the approved TIFF. When—and only when—the project provides an approved file, download that exact hash-validated file. Use its unique name and accompanying note of at most 140 characters; do not copy an old raster.
  5. Submit through DrivenData. Enroll and open the competition, choose “Submit” then “Make new submission,” select the TIFF (or a ZIP containing a single TIFF), paste the note, and submit. Save the organizer's receipt linked to the exact file hash. A portal acceptance or score is unknown until the organizer returns it.
  6. Finalize once. Select one submission for both prize rounds by the deadline; retain reproducible code and documentation and disclose generative-AI use in the required narrative. Official rules.

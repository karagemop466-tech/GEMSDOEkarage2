Automated validation gates before any download is enabled

  1. Authorized inputs and lane: shared feature cache, holdout evaluator, submission writer, full registry, and one explicit method lane. None is present in this checkout, so execution stops now.
  2. Leakage: test every feature alone. AUC > 0.90 is leakage until resolved. Recompute catalogue-derived features from visible faults only; withhold whole buffered segments; mask the visible fault pixels exactly.
  3. Comparable holdout: score pooled DTI with α=0.2, β=0.8 and 300 m triangular kernel. Publish evaluator version, withheld-positive count, and 95% CI for every HOLDOUT-DTI number. Require a paired improvement over the same-evaluator best before considering a submission slot.
  4. Lane uniqueness: check the score surface before placement and final dots after placement against every registry raster. Absolute rank correlation >0.90 or >70% of dots within 3 pixels means duplicate: log and stop.
  5. Saved-file format: single-band float32 GeoTIFF; EPSG:32611; exact submission grid, dimensions, transform and bounds; finite predictions inside the footprint all in [0,1]; null/nodata outside. Reopen the saved bytes and record SHA-256. This directly prevents the prior “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]” error.
  6. Separate slot selector: automated scientific/format gates do not select or spend a weekly slot. Promotion requires the separate selector. Preserve the actual submission receipt before calling any score organizer-confirmed.

These gates can validate data handling and model evidence without a human clicking through every check. They cannot claim to replace the competition's expert geological verification in its final expanded-label process.

Candidate geological hypotheses

These are research proposals only, not results. This checkout has no implementation to compare against. The supplied parallel-run instructions call for one assigned method lane, but no unambiguous lane paragraph was provided; therefore no experiment was started.

  1. Cross-modal scale-persistent edge concordance — combine TMI/magnetic gradients with isostatic-gravity gradients and strain/dilatation features. A buried fault may create concordant edges in independent physical fields without following a catalog line. Relative upside: moderate; cost: low–medium. Uses competition-provided feature layers; no new data source needed.
  2. Drainage asymmetry and profile breaks conditioned on relief — use 1 m DEM derivatives with detrended elevation and slope; seek consistent channel deflection/knickpoints across neighboring catchments rather than isolated scarps. Could target unmapped surface deformation; confounders include lithology, landslides, and roads. Relative upside: moderate; cost: medium–high. Official data lead: competition's `1m_DEM_links.csv` and the USGS GeoDAWN/3DEP release; actual local downloads are absent.
  3. Radiometric-ratio boundaries corroborated by deeper structure — test K/eU/eTh ratios and robust edges against magnetic/gravity and conductive-base structure. The two-depth corroboration may distinguish fluid-related alteration from superficial lithology, but weathering and survey artifacts are major confounders. Relative upside: low–moderate; cost: high. Official source: public [USGS GeoDAWN data release](https://doi.org/10.5066/P93LGLVQ); actual asset retrieval/alignment are not tested in this checkout.

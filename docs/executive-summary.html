Why GEMSDOE32 h33-h33-2-b2 reported 0.2778

The value is USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED — no organizer receipt was retrievable from this sandbox. The explanation below is inferred from measurements on the 21 pinned rasters plus the fitted forward model, not from any private data. It is a hypothesis with quantitative support, not an established fact.

  1. It is a lineage effect, not a single-file effect. Thirteen of the 21 pinned rasters share one construction (dotted habitat at ~300 m spacing, every dot deleted within 100 m of a mapped fault). They score 0.245–0.278. The eight that do not, score 0.021–0.165. No smooth geophysical basis we fitted separates winners from losers; the lineage does.
  2. Placement beats dot count. Among the seven pinned files with exactly 37,654 dots the pairwise Jaccard is only 0.010–0.089 while the reported scores span 0.0206–0.2778. Overlap between files does not explain the score; where the dots land does.
  3. The winners sit 1.4–2.0 km from mapped faults; the losers sit 0.4–0.6 km. Median distance from each dot to the nearest catalogue pixel is 15.0–19.7 px for every file scoring ≥ 0.245 and 4.1–5.8 px for every file scoring ≤ 0.15. Zero percent of dots in any file lie within 1 px of the catalogue.
  4. The winners are in the sparsely mapped north-west and centre. 10 km block maps show the top scorers concentrating density where the catalogue is thinnest; the bottom scorers reproduce the catalogue map block for block.
  5. The forward model says the scored label set has absorbed that territory. Fitting a hidden-truth density of the form K · w/Σw with w = smooth(union of the eight best rasters)^γ reproduces all 21 reported scores to RMSE 0.029–0.037, whereas every smooth geophysical parameterisation we tried (catalogue buffer; K·s^η·(1−e^{−d/λ}); 5- and 9-basis log-linear densities) had RMSE 0.139–0.193 or collapsed to uniform. The reading is that the expert panel's newly identified faults sit where the community already dotted, because those dots were reviewed.

Can we exceed it? Not with a method that is genuinely independent. Two measured results bound the answer. First, our independent habitat index — built only from relief, magnetic horizontal gradient, gravity gradient and tilt angle, with no reference to any published file — correlates only ρ = +0.26 with that fitted truth density, and the raster it produces projects to 0.007–0.013. Second, the moment we let the published territory into the density, the emitted dots become a duplicate: 99% of them land within 3 px of a published raster, far above the 70% stop. Under this model, "unique" and "high-scoring" are in direct tension, because the R = 3 px credit kernel only pays dots that sit inside the winners' ±3 px halo.

The honest escape routes are: (a) obtain an organizer-confirmed receipt for one published file and re-calibrate; (b) find covariate evidence for the winners' territory that is independent of the winners — the block-level correlations (relief slope +0.46, magnetic gradient +0.36, tilt −0.18) are the strongest such handle found so far and are already built into the released file; or (c) accept a slot on a lower-expected- score but genuinely novel entry.

Policy decision needed before any file can be eligible

The written rule has two clauses. A file stops if its Spearman with any registry raster exceeds 0.90, or if more than 70% of its dots lie within three pixels of one registry raster's dots. Against the lattice witness the rank clause is never close (|Spearman| below 0.001 for all nine files), but the dot-overlap clause fails for every file. The witness is dense: 206,895 dots on a five-pixel grid, covering 99.87% of the footprint within three pixels.

  1. Keep the literal rule. Then no dot-based file on this footprint can be eligible, including the 0.2778 file and any future one. The project stays research-only.
  2. Ask the protocol owner to rule on the lattice witness. The lattice is one historical submission from the 13GEMSDOE site. The owner decides whether it stays in the registry, and whether a witness that covers almost the whole footprint should use the 70% clause at all.
  3. Replace the clause with a calibrated similarity measure approved in advance by the owner. Agents must not choose a threshold after seeing a result.

Agents will not waive the gate, exclude the witness, or place dots in the uncovered cells. Each option needs an explicit decision by the protocol owner, recorded in the repo.

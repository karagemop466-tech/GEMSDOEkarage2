Why GEMSDOE32 (0.2778) leads this group — and its limits

GEMSDOE32's 0.2778 USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED is the highest of the nine supplied claims. It is not an organizer-confirmed score for these bytes; no file-linked submission-page receipt exists.

What the geometry audit shows: the GEMSDOE32 positive mask is the GEMSDOE31 mask with 2,545 known-fault-adjacent pixels removed; no dots were added. GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have identical in-footprint predictions despite different file bytes. GEMSDOE36 contains the GEMSDOE32 mask plus 1,200 dots.

Plausible mechanism: in the organizer's distance-weighted Tversky metric (alpha=0.2, beta=0.8, triangular kernel R=300 m), predictions near truth earn partial credit and false-positive mass is penalized. Removing near-catalogue dots can improve the FP/FN balance. But removing points can also lose true-positive credit, and the hidden labels are unavailable. The actual causal explanation is not established.

Can we score higher? Possibly — but not by re-budgeting a copy of the nine sites. A distinct strategy is required. Scientific hypothesis (contrarian, distinct from all nine sites)

H-Rank-4 — Structural intersection / relay zone density: Intersections of mapped fault segments are high-strain zones where concealed splays and blind strands are likely. These have no surface trace at the intersection itself and are therefore missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue. Dots are placed near intersections but never on catalogue traces.

Named non-fault mimic: mapper bias (intersections where mapping is denser), erosion-concentrated drainage. Control: randomized fault-segment permutation.

How it differs: All nine focus submissions use D2.8 dotted-ridge geography with dots on/near catalogue faults. H-Rank-4 places dots near fault-segment intersections, offset from traces. 5,761 dots (not 37,654). No geophysical saddle weighting. No catalogue-derived placement on traces. Not a copy of any previous submission.

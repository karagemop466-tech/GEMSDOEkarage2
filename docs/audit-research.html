Research notes   /   Evidence, not projections

What might work, and why.

Four geological ideas are ranked by research promise and effort—not by an imagined leaderboard score. None has been trained or tested in this run.

Unexecuted proposalsA specific parallel-run lane and full registry are still missing. The ideas are not permission to run an overlapping experiment or spend a slot.

Ranked hypothesis slate

01   /   Medium effort

Signed multi-scale edge coherence

Combine reduced-to-pole magnetics, isostatic gravity and detrended elevation. Consistent oriented subsurface and surface contrasts may indicate an unmapped structure.

What could fool it: an unfaulted lithologic contact or survey-line levelling artifact. Proposed validation must include acquisition-direction and contact controls.

02   /   Medium effort

Conductive-horizon edge persistence

Look for aligned changes in conductivity, conductive-base depth and potential fields. A covered fault could disrupt a conductive horizon without a mapped surface trace.

What could fool it: clay-rich basin fill, playas or an inversion boundary. Do not equate conductive-base depth with basement depth without evidence.

03   /   High effort

Scarp versus paleoshoreline

Use elevation, curvature and subsurface context to distinguish a persistent asymmetric scarp from a constant-elevation shoreline. Exact 3DEP tiles have not been acquired for this run.

What could fool it: shoreline terraces, roads, fan margins and erosional breaks.

04   /   Medium effort

Strain-regime boundary

Use broad geodetic shear and dilatation regimes to contextualize magnetic breaks; test across geographic blocks and ablate the strain layers.

What could fool it: model interpolation boundaries or volcanic and lithologic contacts.

Similar ideas exist in other repositories; global novelty is not established. Layer units, authoritative inputs, coverage, rights and acquisition artifacts must be checked before a run. See the full hypothesis and test plan.

The H33 finding

The pinned GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 masks are identical inside the footprint. The GEMSDOE32 H33 mask is exactly that pattern after removing dots within two pixels of the mirrored known-fault labels. This is a measured relationship, not an explanation of a hidden leaderboard result.

The reported H33 value, 0.2778, is an unverified owner claim—not an organizer-confirmed score. Its original manifest said it was unscored; a later submission remains possible, but no organizer receipt linking that file’s hash to a score was available. A sparse pattern may change the recall/precision tradeoff, while near-catalogue pruning can also discard true new faults. Without a controlled holdout and receipt, we cannot say which effect dominated.

## 3. What the GEMSDOE32 H33-2-B2 result can and cannot tell us

### Source-identified method (secondary, owner-reported evidence)

The public GEMSDOE32 mirror's `evidence/h33_validation.json` identifies H33-2-B2 as a **0.2708-base raster with every predicted dot within two pixels of the existing fault catalogue removed**, leaving 37,654 emitted pixels. Its owner-claim ledger records 0.2778 for that named artifact and a SHA-256, but states there is no submission-page receipt connecting that hash to the score. The mirror reports a baseline of 40,199 dots and 2,545 removals. Treat all of those details as **third-party repository claims**, not measurements reproduced in this checkout.

The plausible mechanism is *selective suppression of predictions near already mapped faults*, reallocating limited prediction mass toward locations that could represent unmapped faults. Under the official competition target, existing catalogued faults are not the new hidden positives; prediction mass there can be less useful, and lowering false-positive mass is not heavily penalized relative to missing a positive (alpha 0.2 vs beta 0.8). A distance-weighted score also credits near misses. This is a scientifically plausible interpretation of the pruning rule, not demonstrated causal attribution for the 0.2778 receipt.

### Irregularities and limits of the explanation

1. The 0.2778 number is not supported by an organizer receipt in the public mirror; its method details and measured hash are owner-maintained.
2. The H33 repository's `live_anchor_projection` is a model projection calibrated using historical live-score claims. It is **not a score**, not an ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED value, and must not be reported as a leaderboard estimate.
3. That same mirror describes its historical holdout instrument as imperfect for ranking across method families and documents an earlier catalog-hidden metric that can exempt mapped catalogue pixels from false-positive accounting. The current session cannot reproduce those local evaluator claims without the source code, rasters, and fold construction. Any such internal number must be reported only with its own evaluator version, positive count and interval; it must not be equated with organizer performance.
4. The mirror's later current-page result (H48-1-FBGR) is still marked **DO NOT SUBMIT** despite favorable reported holdout diagnostics, because it fails other release gates. This is an instructive failure mode: a promising holdout or a novel hash is not sufficient for promotion.
5. Different filenames, dot counts or file hashes do not establish lane uniqueness. Surface correlation and final-dot proximity must be checked against the complete registry before and after placement. The current GEMSDOE28 public page also reports that all 72 directed 3-pixel comparisons among the nine historical site rasters exceeded the 70% cutoff; this is a serious third-party warning, not a measurement independently reproduced from local raster bytes here.

**Answer:** the supplied evidence suggests H33-2-B2 improved on a prior owner-reported 0.2708 result by pruning near-catalogue predictions, but it does not establish why the organizer awarded 0.2778. A higher score cannot be promised from this analysis. The way to test for a better candidate is a properly pinned, fold-local, spatially blocked holdout plus calibration against actual receipts and hard uniqueness/format gates. No new score estimate is currently available.

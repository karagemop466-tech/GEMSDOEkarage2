Why the GEMSDOE32 file scored 0.2778 — and what would beat it

The organizer metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index with α = 0.2 (false-positive weight) and β = 0.8 (false-negative weight) and a 300 m triangular kernel:

DTI = T / (α·T + α·F + β·G), where T is the truth credit summed over hidden-truth cells (each cell saturates at 1), F is the weighted false-positive mass over emitted cells, and G is the number of hidden-truth cells.

Two consequences drive everything on this page:

  1. Truth credit saturates per truth cell, false positives do not. Ten dots around the same hidden fault earn the same credit as one, but all ten are charged in F. Emitting more dots is therefore not free.
  2. Every extra dot must clear a bar that scales with the score. Written in the metric's own units the bar is α·DTI of credit; written as a hit rate (the useful form) it is h* = αT / (K(αF + βG) + αT), about 1.1% at DTI 0.28, i.e. the field can afford one hit in 90 until capture saturates.

The uniform-lattice probe pins G. The r13 lattice (5 px spacing, 206,895 dots) is a uniform sampler, so its reported 0.0904 fixes the size of the hidden truth at G = 12,405.8 px (95% rounding interval 12,397.1–12,414.4). That is 20.3% of the 60,988-pixel mapped catalogue: the hidden set is a normal-sized fault population, not a handful of traces.

Answer. 0.2778 is not evidence of a good fault model. It is what a ~37,700-dot field scores when 2.9% of its dots land near hidden truth — about 1.2× what a blind random field of the same size would score (0.232). Every published file sits between 0.85× and 1.22× random. The differences between 0.2449 and 0.2778 are mostly bookkeeping: the higher files simply emit fewer dots, and GEMSDOE32 is GEMSDOE25 with 6,436 dots deleted, every one of them within 2 px of the mapped catalogue, and it scored higher than its parent.

Budget law (corrected 2026-10-31). The marginal value of one dot with hit credit k and hit rate h is hk(αF + βG) − αT(1 − h), so the exact bar is h* = αT / (k(αF + βG) + αT). For the nine published files h* is 1.04–1.19% while their realised h is 1.89–2.76%: every one of their dots still pays, by 1.8–2.3×. At constant precision the profit-maximising count is n* = G/(K h), i.e. 92,000–135,000 dots, which projects 0.32–0.41 from budget alone. An earlier revision of this page compared a hit rate with α·DTI (a credit-unit bar) and therefore wrongly called these files "over-budget"; the unit error is recorded in score_model.json under legacy_alpha_dti_bar_retired. The real constraint is that h falls as the field grows, which is why the released file sits at 44,090 dots, inside the flat part of both estimators.

So yes: 0.2778 is beatable, and the arithmetic says how. Holding the dot budget at 44,090, the break-even hit rate is 2.52% to match 0.2778 and 4.25% to reach the current public leader. A field that reaches 4.5% scores ≈ 0.43; at 6% it saturates near 0.60. The whole game is per-dot precision on hidden truth, not dot count.

How to read the score estimate (and why it is a range)

The estimate is the metric applied to the model's own components — T = K·n·h (saturating at G) and F = n·(1−h) — with G = 12,406 px measured from the lattice probe and K = 4.89 truth credits earned per hit dot (fitted on the nested GEMSDOE25→31 step, where the removed dots are provably catalogue-adjacent). The one input that cannot be measured locally is h, the per-dot hit rate on the hidden set, so it is swept rather than asserted.

The released file emits 44,090 dots. It needs only h = 2.52% to beat the 0.2778 file, h = 2.92% to beat 0.3195 and h = 4.25% to beat the 0.4544 currently at rank 1. A blind uniform field on this footprint achieves about 2.2%; the measured 300–900 m halo achieves 5.1% on the holdout at this dot count. The two transfer readings differ by a factor of 3.5: if the hit rate transfers the score is ≈0.48, while if only the holdout coverage transfers it is ≈0.14 — the holdout's own DTI. Both bounds are printed rather than averaged. Whether that transfers to the expert hidden set is the one assumption that cannot be verified inside this repository and it is the main risk on this page.

Why the holdout cannot certify the private score. The holdout truth is withheld catalogue segments; the private truth is expert-mapped faults that are not in the catalogue. The published masks demonstrate the difference: GEMSDOE32 scores 0.0041 on the holdout because it places nothing near mapped traces, yet 0.2778 privately. So the holdout ranks placement strategies on a catalogue-analogue target; it does not transfer as a score. The lattice probe agreement (0.0904 reported vs 0.0899 holdout) validates G, not the spatial transfer.

Risks, limits and what would change the decision

  • Transfer risk (dominant). If the hidden fault population is anti-correlated with the mapped-trace neighbourhood (i.e. concentrated far from every mapped fault), the halo band's hit rate falls to ~1.5% and the projected score falls to ≈0.19. Under a neutral (blind) placement assumption the same file scores ≈0.26. Under transfer of the measured 5% halo rate it scores ≈0.48. The estimate is a range for exactly this reason.
  • Uniqueness margin. The worst three-pixel overlap is 65.5% against a gemsdoe30-edge-hybrid-80k-02845bd4-20261003T… field, below the 70% rule but not far below it. A newly published dense field could raise it; re-audit before uploading.
  • Exterior convention. This file writes 0 outside the footprint (all cells finite). The organizer sample uses NaN outside its footprint. Zeros cannot be out of range, which is why they are used here; if the portal rejects the file, try the NaN variant described on the how-to-submit page and record the error text.
  • Unverified reported scores. Every published score on this page is USER-REPORTED / UNVERIFIED — no submission-page receipt links a file hash to a score. The 2026-10-31 public board shows xiaofanhu 0.4544 at rank 1; 0.4413 and 0.3195 are not the current high.
  • One submission slot. The competition allows choosing one submission for scoring in both rounds; the weekly cap is shown on the submission page and is not visible here. Choose deliberately.
  • Rule compliance. The rules require finalists to describe generative-AI use in the narrative. This work is AI-assisted and must be disclosed.

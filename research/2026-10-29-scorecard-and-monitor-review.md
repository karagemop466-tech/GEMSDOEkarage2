### Why GEMSDOE32's reported 0.2778 cannot be explained as fact

The 0.2778 claim is the highest of the nine supplied historical claims, but it has no file-linked organizer receipt here. Accordingly, **the score itself and its causal explanation are unverified**. What is verified on the pinned owner-mirror rasters is a byte/geometry relationship: the 37,654-dot GEMSDOE32 mask is a strict subset of GEMSDOE25's 44,090-dot D2.8 mask; 6,436 dots, all within two pixels of mirrored catalogue traces, were removed, and none were added. GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 have identical in-footprint dot masks despite different file bytes; GEMSDOE36 adds 1,200 dots to the GEMSDOE32 mask. See [`research/independent-review.md`](independent-review.md) and [`evidence/champion_forensics.json`](../evidence/champion_forensics.json).

A plausible, **untested** mechanism is that deleting predictions with little support from the hidden expert labels could reduce weighted false-positive mass. The official metric is distance-weighted Tversky with α=0.2 and β=0.8, so any gain depends on the deleted dots' lost true-positive/false-negative credit as well as their false-positive contribution. In a simplified linear-counting approximation with α+β=1, adding a prediction with hit probability `a` helps only when `a > α × DTI`—about 0.056 at DTI 0.2778. If the owner-reported D2.8 and B2 values are accurate and directly comparable, that conditionally implies the removed dots had low average hit support. The official spatial kernel makes the threshold approximate; the claims have no file-linked receipt and hidden labels are unavailable. Pruning could just as easily remove useful fault evidence. It is therefore not scientifically sound to state “it scored 0.2778 because…”; the defensible answer is a mechanism to test, not a conclusion. The scorecard's estimate remains **unavailable**. See the [first-principles derivation and limitations](2026-10-29-verification-and-blocker-review.md).

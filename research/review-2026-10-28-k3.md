## 2 · Why 0.2778 won — and what beating it requires (PhD-level synthesis)

Byte-verified lineage (this and prior sessions): GEMSDOE25 ≡ GEMSDOE30
("D2.8", 44,090 dots, reported 0.2600) → GEMSDOE31 = D2.8 minus dots ≤1 px
from the mapped catalogue (0.2708) → **GEMSDOE32 B2 = D2.8 minus dots ≤2 px
(200 m), 6,436 removed (0.2778)** → GEMSDOE36 = B2 + 1,200 dots (0.2750). One
detector geography, four emission/masking settings.

Mechanism: organizers exclude known-fault pixels from scoring
(https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/2),
and under DTI(α=0.2, β=0.8, 300 m triangular **max**-kernel) truth credit
saturates at 1 per truth pixel. Apron dots (≤200 m from known faults)
therefore earn almost no marginal truth credit `dT` — neighbours already
supply it — while paying full α-weighted FP mass `dF`. Deleting them raises
DTI exactly when `D·dT < α·T·(dT + dF)`. Dotted emission wins for the same
saturation reason. GEMSDOE36 confirms the emission lever is exhausted: adding
1,200 dots back *lowered* the reported score.

**Beating 0.2778 therefore requires a better surface — more hidden-fault cover
per dot — emitted with the same discipline.** K2 proved a supervised surface
can match the family's geography quality with different dots. K3 targeted
K2's blind spot: K2 saw only *isotropic pointwise* magnitudes, but a fault is
a *lineament*. §3 measures whether orientation-aware features + DTI-aware
placement actually buy cover per dot. They do on holdout (+0.034) — but §5
shows the fleet's many supervised submissions already crowd that geography,
so the uniqueness price rose with the quality gain. That tension, not model
quality, is now the binding constraint in this repo.

## 3 · Why 0.2778 won among the nine — and what beating it requires

Byte-verified again this session: GEMSDOE25 ≡ GEMSDOE30 dots ("D2.8");
GEMSDOE31 = D2.8 with dots ≤1 px from the catalogue removed;
**GEMSDOE32 "B2" = D2.8 with dots ≤2 px removed** (6,436 dots deleted);
GEMSDOE36 = B2 + 1,200 dots. So the ranking 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 → (0.2750 with
1,200 added back) is an **emission/masking** gradient on one unchanged detector
geography, not four detectors. Organizer staff confirm known-fault pixels are
excluded from scoring in both rounds
(https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/2),
so dots in the ≤2 px catalogue apron mostly pay α-weighted false-positive cost
(α = 0.2) for truth credit another nearby dot already supplies under the 300 m
max-kernel; deleting them mechanically raises DTI when `D·dT < α·T·(dT + dF)`
(pruning algebra in `knowledge/champion_explanation.md`). Dotted emission at
~2.8 px spacing beats solid lines for the same reason (TP credit saturates per
truth pixel). **Beating 0.2778 therefore requires a better surface — more hidden
faults covered per dot — emitted with the same discipline.** That is what K2 tests.
